---
title: "Inclusion"
intro: >
  Inclusion means access for all. It means recognising the differences between
  people and providing opportunities for everyone to take part in running,
  whatever those differences — as a runner, a leader, an official or a
  volunteer.
---

Chard Road Runners embraces diversity and difference, and is committed to
providing opportunities that are safe, inclusive, accessible and equitable. We
want the club to be equally open to everyone, whatever their age, disability,
gender, race, ethnicity, sexuality or social and economic status.

We comply with the [Equality Act 2010](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/equality-act-2010-guidance)
and the characteristics protected by it, and we encourage our members to do the
same. We aim to include everyone, whether or not they have a protected
characteristic.

## Our aim

We want an environment where everyone feels:

- welcome
- represented
- included in decision making
- able to take part
- safe, and free from discrimination, bullying and harassment

## What we commit to

- We will not tolerate discrimination, harassment, bullying or victimisation.
- We will actively identify and reduce barriers to taking part for
  under-represented groups.
- We will make sure under-represented groups have the opportunity to take part
  in all aspects of the club.
- We will provide opportunities for all in coaching, leading and officiating.

## A welcoming environment

We will think about how we can include people, rather than focusing on barriers.
We will consider how the club is promoted — providing information in accessible
formats and using appropriate imagery.

We encourage anyone to contact us to discuss what they need in order to take
part, and we will consider what reasonable adjustments could be made. Where
adjustments are needed to make a session or event accessible, we will make them.

We will not make assumptions. We would rather talk to you about what would help.

## The limits of what we can offer

The scope, training and qualifications of our run leaders, and the facilities
available to the club, may limit our ability to support every disability. Where
that's the case we will say so honestly rather than leave you to find out.

## If you have a concern

If you feel you have been treated unfairly at the club, speak to any committee
member, or contact our [welfare officer](/welfare). Complaints about the
behaviour of members are handled through the disciplinary process set out in the
[Rules and Constitution](/rules-and-constitution).
