If you've been wondering where my high quality, witty banter race reports have gone, I've been on a brief hiatus since Neroche 10k, because it's hard to dedicate time to talking about running when you can't go running. But I'm now back and will endeavour to bring you the latest race reports a bit closer to when they actually happened 🤣.

Helen has shared some fantastic reports in my absence, and the first one off my rank will be to complete her untitled report from the Cider Challenge weekend.

Whilst Helen told you about what happened in the 'shorter' races, you'll have missed out on what Andy and Paul got up to over some longer distances. Here's Paul's download from the weekend.

> This weekend was another great one for CRRs in the heat of the day and night. Andrew Leeson kicked off the weekend of running with a 20:00 start on the Friday evening. Andrew's plan: get off the start line and see what happens!
>
> With the cooler hours ahead the decision was made to keep running until sleep deprivation started to take its toll, having worked all day, and with a CRR HQ set up Andrew started to tick off the 5.4k-ish laps. At 05:30 I looked out the window of my camper to see Andrew making great steady pace, having only stopped to top up refreshments.
>
> As the heat of the early morning sun came into play Andrew's goal was set to achieve 60 miles, effectively 19 laps. My race was fast approaching so I grabbed a few photos and then it was off. I had a goal of marathon distance, although with little training and the heat I had my concerns, but with a 12 hour cutoff this was definitely achievable.
>
> Keeping a sharp eye out for Andrew I completed my first lap to see him head down sleeping well, so I took off to continue my laps through the varied undulations and crops that made running loops more interesting.
>
> With the half-marathon distance in the bag, I headed out on a reverse loop with my camera to photograph as many runners as possible. Andrew was in fine form and I was able to witness one runner posing well and then falling with what can only be described as a full on professional roll out — all missed by my camera!
>
> Getting back to HQ it was time for a quick refreshment and both Andrew and I decided to do a lap, Andrew's last, together as the heat of the day was definitely taking its toll. Lap completed, Andy settled down for some much needed sleep and I cracked on in the better anti-clockwise direction again to roll out my lap to complete my marathon.
>
> Andy's miles came out in the results as 63, whilst mine worked out as 28. So-ish mile laps it was! Andrew showed great resilience and endurance in a fantastic achievement in less than ideal conditions.
